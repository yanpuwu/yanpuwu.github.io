Expositions

Expository writing, seminar notes, and reading reports by Yanpu Wu

This page collects seminar notes, reading reports prepared for courses, and independent expositions (which were mostly written in my freshman and sophomore years, focusing on some simple algebraic facts or problems).

Some of these writing were originally in Chinese if there is Chinese version available. The English version for these notes were translated and organized by Chatgpt and I haven’t had time to check them.

Info

Reading Reports

Poisson Deformation of del Pezzo Surfaces

English PDF

Info

UChicago Math REU 2026, advised by Victor Ginzburg

This is the final survey paper of math REU in UChicago during 2026 summer. Advised by Prof. Victor Ginzburg, I read the paper Noncommutative Del Pezzo Surfaces and Calabi-Yau Algebras by P. Etingof and V. Ginzburg.

The whole reading project covers the main results of this paper, including the proof and construction of the noncommutative deformation of a del Pezzo surface by using the tools of Calabi-Yau algebras, admitting Kontsevich’s formality theorem of which I have no opportunity to learn the details within a summer. However, due to page limit of the final paper I can only cover a part of the result in the original paper, that concerns the “quasi-classical” degeneration of the noncommutative story, that is, the deformation of the Poisson structure.

Deligne’s 5 Decompositions

English PDF

Info

Course: Topics in Algebraic Geometry (Peverse Sheaves and BBDG Decomposition)

This is the final survey paper of a course on perverse sheaves and BBDG decomposition taught by Prof. Qizheng Yin in 2025 Fall semester. I read the paper Hodge-theoretic Splitting Mechanisms for Projective Maps by M.A.A. de Cataldo and Decompositions dans la categorie dérivée by P. Deligne.

The survey summarizes five different decompositions induced by the isomorphism given by relative hard Lefschetz under an abstract setting as discussed in de Cataldo’s paper and calculates some concrete examples in the language of matrix. The most important part is the elegant and ``self-dual” decomposition as constructed by Deligne in his paper.

Hilbert 90 for K2

English PDF

Info

Course: Selected Topics in Algebraic Geometry (Algebraic cycles)

This is the final survey paper of a course on algebraic cycles and K-theoretic methods taught by Prof. Zhiyu Tian in 2025 Fall semester. I read a part of the paper Hilbert’s Theorem 90 for \(𝛫_2\), with Application to the Chow Groups of Rational Surfaces by J.-L. Colliot-Thélène.

The survey starts with a summary on some basic K-theoretic results that is used in the proof, together with the idea of a specialization argument. Geometric Littlewood-Richardson Rule

Info

Course: Algebraic Combinatorics

This is the final survey paper (joint with Fenglin Yu) of a course on algebraic combinatorics taught by Prof. Yibo Gao in 2024 Fall semester. We read a part of the paper A Geometric Littlewood-Richardson Rule by R. Vakil.

Independent Expositions

Equivalence between Flat, Projective and Locally Free Modules

English PDF Chinese version

Info

This note records a proof of the standard commutative-algebra problem of understanding when finite flatness, projectivity, and local freeness describe the same class of modules. It proves five equivalent formulations under finite-presentation hypotheses, moving between global and local statements through localization, Nakayama’s lemma, the behavior of Hom under base change, and faithfully flat descent. The presentation follows the corresponding discussion in Algebraic Geometry and Commutative Algebra by S. Bosch.

Calculation of Two Galois Group

English PDF Chinese version

Info

This note began with two exercises from N. Jacobson’s Basic Algebra concerning generic trinomials in positive characteristic. For \(x^p-sx-t\) and \(x^{p+1}-sx-t\), it computes the splitting-field degrees and identifies the resulting Galois actions with \(\mathrm{AGL}_1(\mathbb F_p)\) and \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\). The first calculation uses differences of roots, while the second introduces a normalized cross-ratio coordinate to make the projective action transparent. The results originate in Kôji Uchida’s work on general trinomials.

Hua Lo Keng Theorems

English PDF Chinese version

Info

This note continues a group of exercises in Jacobson’s Basic Algebra I, centered on results of Hua Lo-Keng. It includes a classical concise proof of the Cartan–Brauer–Hua theorem, starting from Hua’s identities. And I also present my own proof of Hua’s theorem on additive inverse-preserving maps between division rings based on the identities together with a local-to-global argument for multiplicativity. It also compares Brauer’s alternative proof and briefly explains the connection with projective geometry and von Staudt’s theorem.

A Double Cover of SO(4)

English PDF Chinese version

Info

This note addresses the classical description of \(\mathrm{SO}(4)\) through unit quaternions. It studies the left-right action \(v\mapsto\alpha v\bar{\beta}\) and proves that \(S^3\times S^3\to\mathrm{SO}(4)\) is a surjective homomorphism with kernel \(\{(1,1),(-1,-1)\}\). My own proof presented in this note is brutal and purely algebraic.
